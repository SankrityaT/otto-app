# Pitch brief

Everything the deck needs in one place: the story, the facts with their sources, what makes Otto different, a 5-slide outline, the 30-second demo and the visuals to use. Every number here comes from [`research.md`](research.md); please don't add figures that aren't in it.

**Links:** app [ottocoach.vercel.app/app](https://ottocoach.vercel.app/app) · landing page [ottocoach.vercel.app](https://ottocoach.vercel.app) · code [github.com/SankrityaT/otto-app](https://github.com/SankrityaT/otto-app)

## The one-liner

**Otto turns any hard job task into one small step at a time, shown in 3D and talked through out loud, and connects students with a raft of peers who've done it, so they need help less and less.**

Tagline (from Havi's landing page): *Big tasks, one small step at a time.*

## Who it's for

Autistic high school students and young adults up to 22 in transition programs, learning real job tasks at school work sites (at AZACS: grades 9–12, plus a Transition Academy for ages 18–22). Our partner school, Arizona Autism Charter Schools (AZACS), runs a café, a shirt studio and a shipping store for this. At school there are about 3 students per staff member; at a first job, nobody prompts each step.

## The problem, in three beats

1. **Job tasks have many steps, and staff prompt every one.** AZACS's own goal for students is *"demonstrating the need for fewer prompts."* At a first job, those prompts disappear.
2. **There aren't enough people to give one-to-one help.** Job coaches are "essential" for many autistic workers, but there aren't enough good ones (Buckland Review 2024). 90% of US disability service providers report staffing problems (ANCOR 2024). In the words of a CoSN spokesperson: *"a teacher can't go around and do all those adaptations in the classroom of 45 students all on their own."* (Paykamian, 2021)
3. **Generic AI makes it worse, not better.** Practitioners reviewing chatbot answers for autistic adults found them *"wordy, vague and overwhelming"* (Carik et al., CHI 2025). Plain AI help cut exam scores by 17% once it was taken away (Bastani et al., PNAS 2025): students learned to lean on it. And 23% of vulnerable children who use chatbots say it's because they have no one else to talk to (Internet Matters 2025).

## What Otto is

**It works on the device the school already has.** Phone, iPad, Chromebook or laptop: point its camera at the desk and Otto sees the work. AZACS already bought 350+ Chromebooks and gives iPads to students who need them (Paykamian, 2021), and a nonverbal student's *"iPad is actually their voice."* Nothing new to buy, and nothing to wear for students (adults can choose camera glasses).

**The student says what they're doing, Otto coaches it step by step:**

- Breaks the task into small physical steps, one on screen at a time.
- Shows each step as a **diagram straight away, then a 3D model** the student can turn in their hand.
- **Talks the student through it** with a natural voice, and answers questions out loud ("is this the right way up?") from what the camera sees.
- Notices when a step is done, without the student having to tap.
- Gives more help **only when asked**, and **less help every time** the student succeeds. It follows First Place AZ's five stages: watch, first tries, with help, check-in, on your own.

**The Raft: real peers, not a chatbot.** A small, closed group from the same work site, set up by staff. Students see each other's otters in a shared pond: who's working, who needs help, who just finished. They help each other with the same 3D models, work side by side ("do it together"), and cheer wins. No video, no strangers, no rankings, no streaks.

## What makes Otto different (the moats)

| | Everyone else | Otto |
|---|---|---|
| **Complex things made simple** | Text answers, or videos someone recorded in advance | A 3D model of *your* step, made from the task, that you turn in your hand, plus one step at a time out loud |
| **Peers** | An AI that becomes the friend | Help is routed back to **real raftmates who've mastered that step**. Peer-based instruction is an evidence-based practice (AFIRM/NCAEP), and autistic-to-autistic information transfer works as well as between non-autistic people (Crompton et al. 2020) |
| **Independence** | More use = more dependence | Help **fades by design**. Success is measured the way the school measures it: fewer prompts |
| **Hardware** | Headsets, glasses, new devices | Any device's camera, and a layer other hardware plugs into: any camera or big button that speaks two small endpoints (the otter, a document camera, an accessibility switch) gets the same coaching. AR/VR *"can cause sensory overload"* for autistic students (Paykamian, 2021), and AZACS staff told us anything worn is a sensory problem |

What to say out loud: **"Other AI tools answer the student. Otto makes the student need it less, and connects them to each other."**

## Competitors

| | Who it's for | What it does | Where Otto is different |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ama** (withama.ai) | Autistic children 3–17 | Screen-only: social stories, reading, communication. $20/month | Otto is high school to age 22, hands-on job tasks, checks real work with a camera, and fades help |
| **Focusmate** | Adults (mostly ADHD) | Live video with a stranger to work side by side ("body doubling") | Otto's "do it together" has no video and no strangers, and each otter moves only as real steps get done |
| **General chatbots** | Everyone | Answer questions in text | Otto coaches physical steps, shows them in 3D, checks the real work and points students to peers |

## Privacy and safety (judges will ask)

- The camera only looks when needed (when a step is checked or the student asks), and photos are **never stored**. A live "what Otto sees" window is always on screen.
- Otto won't help with weapons, self-harm or hurting anyone, and the server enforces it: unsafe tasks get no steps, and a student who sounds at risk is always told to talk to staff and given the 988 line.
- The Raft is closed (staff set it up, no strangers, no location, no direct messages, no video). Every post is checked for kindness and personal info, and swear words are blocked. One tap reports or blocks.
- No streaks, no leaderboards, nothing red.

## The future: the Otto lamp

A desk lamp with a wide-angle camera looking straight down at the work, a speaker and a mic. Not worn. It talks the student through the task in real time, and a small projector **points at the real part with light** ("this one, push until it clicks"). The 3D model stays on the screen. Keep the light dim and steady for sensory comfort.

The printed desk otter in the repo is a first step toward this: a camera in a pebble with a privacy shutter you can see and feel.

5. **Evidence, partners and what's next.** AZACS and First Place AZ, 3–4 research facts from above, privacy in one line, and the Otto lamp as the next step.

## The 30-second demo (shot list)

1. **0–3 s:** A student points a phone or iPad at their desk: *"Help me put the camera board in my otter."*
2. **3–15 s:** Otto breaks it into steps; each step shows as a diagram, then a **3D model** the student can turn, while Otto explains it out loud.
3. **15–22 s:** The student asks out loud, *"Which way does the antenna go?"*, and Otto answers by voice from what the camera sees.
4. **22–27 s:** The Raft: Ava's otter sits up, "Can someone help?". Another student taps her and sends the same 3D model. A cheer pops.
5. **27–30 s:** The ring fills: "6 of 6 on my own." Tagline.
